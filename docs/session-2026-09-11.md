## Merging articles ignored the mill and the money

- Owner: "how can you put Canclini trouser with Zegna and Loro Piana, knowing
  Canclini doesn't have 71% Wool 15% Silk 14% Linen fabrics?"
- The line merge key was garment + fibre + gsm only. Fabric number was never
  in it and `includeFabricBrand` defaulted off, so 17 different Loro Piana
  cloths collapsed into one article, and one worksheet row spanned Zegna,
  Canclini and Solbiati. `ignoreUnitPrice: true` then pulled rows with
  different money together and `mergeConsolidationGroup` wrote price 0.
- The rule is now the owner's: same garment, composition, weight, **mill**
  and **fabric price** merge; anything else keeps its own line.
  `includeFabricCost` joins `includeFabricBrand` in `ConsolidationOptions`,
  and `reductionOptions` turns both on instead of ignoring price.
- `costHintInvoiceGroupKey` gained the mill and all three money columns.
  Rows with no fibre or no weight still key to null and never merge.
- `buildCostHintWorksheetFromInvoice` took a single `salesOrder`, so a
  combined invoice covering 7 orders could only resolve fabric details for
  the first. It now takes `salesOrders[]` and the loader passes every
  covered order.
- "Canclini" on these rows is not Canclini cloth: it is the display alias
  for HAGAN warehouse linen (legacy `gliani-stock` / `gliani-warehouse`).
- Consolidation rewrites and **saves** invoice lines, so invoices merged
  under the old key keep the flattened lines. `POST /api/customer-invoices/
  [id]/sync-lines` rebuilds them from the orders; the invoice page now has
  a "Rebuild lines from sales orders" button because that API had no UI.
- `cost-hint-worksheet.test.ts` was in no npm script; it now runs inside
  `test:consolidate-lines`.
